# Legal Audit — 2026-09-02

First pass. Scope: the three public legal pages, the marketing copy that makes
legal claims, the Stripe money flow, and consent capture. Source-read, not
live-site read.

Severity: 🔴 fix before another course signs · 🟠 fix before scale · 🟡 track

---

## 🔴 L-1 — The Operator Agreement promises a payout the code does not deliver

**Contract says** (`src/app/operator-agreement/page.tsx:35, 46`):
> "GreenReserve's only fee is the $1.50 per-player service fee charged to the
> golfer, **never deducted from what you receive**."
> "GreenReserve collects its own $1.50 per-player service fee **as a separate
> charge to the golfer; it is not part of your payout**."

**Marketing says** (`HomeContent.tsx:219`, `ForCoursesContent.tsx:559`):
> "Green fees hit your Stripe account directly — no holding period, no revenue
> share." · "**it never touches your Stripe account**."

**Code does** (`src/lib/checkin-booking.ts:102-107` → `src/lib/stripe.ts:50-62`):
one **direct charge on the course's connected account** for
`booking.totalAmount` (green fee + cart + the $1.50s), with GreenReserve's cut
taken as `application_fee_amount`. Confirmed by the comment at
`src/lib/platform-stripe.ts:5-7`.

**Why it matters.** On a direct charge the connected account is merchant of
record and bears Stripe's processing fee — on the *full* amount, including
GreenReserve's fee. So:

> 4 players × $60 green fee = $240, plus $6 service fee = **$246 charged**.
> Stripe ≈ 2.9% + $0.30 = **$7.43**. Application fee = **$6.00**.
> Course receives **$232.57** against an explicit written promise of $240.

The fee is not "separate," it is not "never deducted," and it very much
"touches your Stripe account." Three sentences in an executed contract and two
in live marketing are each falsifiable against the code by anyone who opens
their Stripe dashboard.

**Two ways out, pick one — they are not equivalent:**

- **(A) Change the words.** Keep direct charges. Rewrite the promise as what
  is actually true: *"Your green fee is never reduced by GreenReserve's fee.
  Stripe's standard processing fee applies to the transaction, as it would with
  any processor."* Cheapest, honest, and slightly less good as a sales pitch.
- **(B) Change the money.** Charge the golfer's $1.50 as a **second, separate
  PaymentIntent on the GreenReserve platform account**, leaving the course's
  charge at exactly the green fee. Then the copy becomes true as written. Costs
  a second Stripe fee ($0.30 fixed on $6 is brutal — ~7% of the fee) and adds a
  failure mode where one charge succeeds and the other doesn't.

⚖️ COUNSEL not required to choose, but the rewritten language in (A) is worth
a review pass before it goes live to more courses.

## 🔴 L-2 — Public pages assert an LLC that does not exist

`src/app/privacy/page.tsx:147` and `src/app/operator-agreement/page.tsx:148`
both render:

> "GreenReserve is operated by `{{COMPANY_LEGAL_NAME}}`, a limited liability
> company formed in the state of New Jersey."

Two defects in one sentence: the placeholder renders **literally** on the live
page, and the claim is **false** — no LLC has been formed.

**Why it matters.** Stating you are an LLC when you are not is a
misrepresentation to every course that signs. It is also the wrong direction of
error: it invites a court to find you *personally* liable while you get none of
the protection you claimed. Separately, an unformed entity means the Operator
Agreement's counterparty is **Cam Sanchez, individually** — personal assets are
behind every course contract, every golfer's card charge, and every
late-cancellation fee dispute.

**Fix now, before the LLC exists:** replace with *"GreenReserve is operated by
Cam Sanchez, sole proprietor, in New Jersey."* True, unglamorous, and it stops
the misrepresentation today. Swap in the entity the day the filing is stamped.

## 🔴 L-3 — The Operator Agreement's automatic service-fee refund is not implemented

**Contract says** (`operator-agreement/page.tsx:57`):
> "GreenReserve's service fee is **refunded automatically** whenever the
> underlying green-fee charge is refunded in full."

**Code does** (`src/lib/stripe.ts:66-76`): `refundOnConnectedAccount` calls
`stripe.refunds.create` **without `refund_application_fee: true`**. On a direct
charge, Stripe does not return the application fee unless you ask. A full
refund therefore leaves the course out of pocket by the $1.50s while
GreenReserve keeps them.

One-line fix in the code, or delete the promise. Not both.

## 🟠 L-4 — Undisclosed $0.50 membership fee

`src/lib/stripe.ts:12` defines `MEMBERSHIP_FEE_CENTS = 50`, taken as an
application fee on membership-dues charges (`api/membership/[id]/route.ts:93`).

It appears in **no** public document — not `/terms`, not `/privacy`, not
`/operator-agreement`, not `/for-courses`. Meanwhile the Operator Agreement
says GreenReserve's *"only fee"* is the $1.50 per player.

An undisclosed fee that contradicts an "only fee" clause is the cleanest
possible fact pattern for a deceptive-practices complaint. Disclose it in the
Operator Agreement fee section, or remove it from the code.

## 🟠 L-5 — SMS consent is implied, and there is no revocation record

`/terms` and `/privacy` both take the position that *using the service* is
consent to transactional SMS. Twilio is wired (`src/lib/twilio.ts`,
`two-factor.ts`, `golfer/auth/otp/request`). There is **no `STOP` handler and no
revocation field anywhere in the schema** — grep found none.

Current law, verified 2026-09-02:

- The FCC's **consent-revocation rule** (effective 2025-04-11) requires honoring
  a revocation made by **any reasonable means** within **10 business days**, and
  the revocation must extend across message types. Twilio's default `STOP`
  handling covers the keyword path only — it does **not** cover a golfer
  replying "please stop texting me" in words, or emailing
  hello@greenreserve.app. Those are reasonable means and currently go nowhere.
- The Fifth Circuit rejected the FCC's *prior express written consent* rule in
  March 2026 — but that is **Texas/Louisiana/Mississippi only**. GreenReserve
  sits in the Third (NJ) and Fourth (SC) Circuits, where the FCC's written-
  consent framework still governs. Do not read the Fifth Circuit case as relief.
- Purely transactional/informational texts sit on a softer footing than
  marketing, and GreenReserve is disciplined about the marketing line in
  writing. That is the thing protecting you here — protect it in code too.

**Why it matters more than it looks:** TCPA damages are **$500–$1,500 per
message**, statutory, no injury required, and the plaintiff's bar runs it as an
industry. A booking reminder loop that texts four players is four exposures per
booking.

Work items: add a revocation field + honor path, a `STOP`/`HELP` handler of your
own, and an audit trail of when consent was given (you already stamp
`termsVersion` at booking — extend the same idea to comms).

## 🟡 L-6 — Assent is recorded on the client's word, not the server's

**Correction to an earlier draft of this finding:** the notice placement is
*fine*. `src/app/book/page.tsx:449-451` renders "By confirming, you agree to
GreenReserve's Terms of Service and this course's cancellation policy"
**immediately above** the confirm button at `:452`. That is a textbook
enforceable sign-in wrap — conspicuous, adjacent, linked. Leave it alone.

What remains is narrower: `termsAccepted: true` is a **hardcoded client-side
literal** (`:399`, `:497`), so the server records assent because the browser
said so, not because it observed anything. If a golfer later disputes that they
saw the terms — which is the fight an arbitration clause exists to win — the
evidence is a boolean the client supplied.

You already stamp `termsVersion` server-side at `api/bookings/route.ts:225`,
which is most of the value. The gap is that nothing records *that the notice was
rendered*. Low severity, cheap to close: derive assent server-side from the
request rather than trusting the flag, and keep the version stamp as the
audit record.

## 🟠 L-7 — Unilateral amendment clause contradicts itself

`operator-agreement/page.tsx` changelog: *"Operators who already accepted an
earlier version are not required to re-accept — this version applies going
forward **and to any future disputes on the same terms as before**."*

That sentence says two opposite things, and the half that matters — binding an
operator to terms they never saw — is the half that is weakest. You already have
version-stamping machinery (`CURRENT_TERMS_VERSION`). Use it: re-prompt operators
on a material change. With fewer than ten courses this costs one email.

## 🟢 What is actually fine

Worth stating so effort goes where it matters:

- **Money transmission: clean.** Direct charges with the connected account as
  merchant of record means GreenReserve never takes custody of course funds.
  This is the structure that keeps you out of money-transmitter licensing. Do
  not casually change it — see the escalation trigger in `README.md`.
- **PCI scope: minimal.** Stripe Elements + SetupIntent, tokens only, no PAN on
  GreenReserve servers. Privacy policy describes this accurately.
- **Off-session authorization: present.** `usage: 'off_session'` on the
  SetupIntent, matching disclosure in `/terms` under card authorization. Better
  than most startups at this stage.
- **No third-party tracking.** No GA, no pixels, no session replay. No cookie
  banner needed, and the "we don't sell your data" claim is actually true.
- **State privacy laws: almost certainly not applicable yet.** The comprehensive
  state regimes gate on ~100k consumers or ~$25M revenue. The exceptions are
  Texas and Nebraska, which have no volume threshold but exempt SBA-defined
  small businesses — GreenReserve is comfortably inside that. Revisit at the
  scale trigger; do not build a DSAR pipeline now.
