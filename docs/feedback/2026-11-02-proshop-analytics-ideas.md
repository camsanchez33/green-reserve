# Pro shop and Analytics ideas and feedback (saved for later, 2026-11-02)

The second part of the outside review Cam pasted (the first part is in
`2026-11-02-dashboard-ideas.md`), saved to go over later. Nothing here is decided.
The text below is the review, condensed; the opinions are the reviewer's, not Cam's.

## Overall
- Further along than the reviewer expected. Analytics is the strongest page
  conceptually; the pro shop has the right foundation.
- Make the pages deeper rather than adding random features or redesigning; the
  layout, type, green branding and navigation stay.
- Three things that would make GreenReserve compelling: tee sheet management,
  financial intelligence (how the course makes money and where it loses
  chances), and customer intelligence (bring golfers back, raise spend per
  golfer). "Don't just tell courses what happened — help them decide what to
  do next."

## 1. Pro shop — a real retail POS
A. Product area (currently a lot of unused space, very basic cards):
   - product images, name, price, stock count, quick add;
   - variants (size, colour) and a small low-stock indicator;
   - a 3–4 column grid instead of narrow cards;
   - one click adds a dozen golf balls.

B. Checkout panel — the current "This sale" box is too small. It wants a
   persistent, prominent panel with:
   - items, quantity, line prices, subtotal, tax, discounts and total;
   - the payment method, a Complete sale button, and quick Cash / Card /
     Member account.

C. Today's sales:
   - add who rang it up, payment method, type (shop item, round, round pack),
     total, receipt and refund;
   - each sale opens a detail panel.

D. Bug it spotted: the Logo polo tile says "none left" while the Stock section
   says "1 low: Polo" — make stock status agree in both places.

## 2. Analytics — business intelligence
Liked: revenue, tee sheet filled, rounds played, average per round, bookings,
no-shows, cancellations, open spots and cart usage — "not just revenue".

A. Top numbers:
   - revenue against last year;
   - utilisation plus the capacity still available;
   - rounds against the previous period;
   - revenue per round with its trend;
   - later, a fifth number for pro shop revenue.

B. Revenue chart:
   - hover shows the exact amount; click a day for its transactions;
   - daily, weekly and monthly views;
   - toggle revenue, rounds or utilisation;
   - compare with last week, last month or last year.

C. Revenue breakdown: green fees, carts, pro shop, events, memberships and other
   fees, as a donut plus a table. Its start is already in Monthly review.

D. Golfers: four groups — regulars, new, at risk (were regular, stopped) and
   inactive (none in a set period). Click a group to see its golfers, and later
   send targeted promotions to the inactive.

E. Heat map: keep it, add hover detail (utilisation, rounds), click through to
   those tee times, and a toggle between "how full" and "revenue per available
   spot".

## 3. Across the platform
- **Notification center** — one place for declined payments, low stock,
  upcoming events, cancellations and operational alerts.
- **Global search** — golfers, transactions, bookings, products and events.
- **Activity history** — who changed a booking, refunded, changed a price or
  stock.
- **Phone and tablet staff mode** — check in, look up and take payments.

## 4. Things it noticed on the Analytics screenshot (accuracy over looks)
- "100% came back" — define "came back" and the period.
- "228 bookings" vs "539 rounds" — say bookings are reservations and rounds are
  golfers.
- "1,082 open spots" — player spaces or tee times?
- "100 tee times sold out" — show which dates and times.
- "$0 shop items on rounds" — make it an opportunity metric (the potential
  extra sales).
- Every percentage needs a defined denominator and comparison period.

## 5. Its suggested order
**Phase 1 — improve existing pages:**
- pro shop product grid;
- a proper checkout panel;
- transaction detail views;
- analytics chart interactions;
- a revenue breakdown visual;
- fix metric definitions and consistency.

**Phase 2 — differentiation:**
- golfer CRM and retention;
- automated marketing and promotions;
- tee-sheet demand forecasting;
- employee activity and reporting;
- AI business insights.
