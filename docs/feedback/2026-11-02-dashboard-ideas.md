# Dashboard ideas and feedback (saved for later, 2026-11-02)

Cam pasted this from another assistant's review of the new dashboard, to be saved
and discussed later. Not a plan or a spec, and nothing here is decided. The text
below is that review, condensed; the scores and opinions are its, not Cam's.

## Overall
- Reads as a legitimate golf-course management platform. Prefers the dark
  version as the more premium look and would make dark the default, keeping
  light available. Scored the direction 8.5/10 (visual consistency 9,
  navigation 9, hierarchy 8, premium feel 8.5, operational usefulness 8.5).
- Liked: the sidebar, the green-vs-grey bar chart, "Needs you" (declined cards
  with Text pay link), "Next 14 days", and the typography.
- Advice: don't make every page an analytics dashboard. The tee sheet should
  feel like scheduling software, the pro shop like a retail POS, Money like
  financial software, Analytics like BI — all in one design language.

## Home: priorities it listed
1. More visual hierarchy — the day's most important numbers stand out.
2. Payment alerts — tell overdue payments apart from recently declined cards.
3. Revenue and rounds played on the main dashboard.
4. Course conditions card with more visual presence.
5. Setup checklist collapsible once setup is finished.
6. The floating assistant better integrated into the interface.

It wants Home to be an operating center answering: how many golfers today,
revenue so far, groups still owing, staffing or scheduling problems, and how
busy the next two weeks look. It would expand "Next 14 days" into a defining
feature.

## Eight broader directions
1. **Daily-operations Home** — bigger revenue and rounds numbers with % change,
   more prominent action alerts, a cleaner daily activity feed, and a compact
   setup checklist.
2. **Tee sheet** — timeline of times, colour-coded statuses, names and group
   sizes, carts, paid or unpaid on each booking, drag and drop.
3. **Pro shop POS** — product photo grid, categories, cart on the right, quick
   member lookup, automatic member discounts, split payments, saved receipts.
4. **Events center** — name, date, registered, spots left, registration
   revenue, format, sponsors; an event-creation flow with a live preview of the
   registration page.
5. **Revenue intelligence** — daily, weekly and monthly revenue; tee time vs pro
   shop; revenue per round (singled out); average transaction; revenue by
   payment method; comparison with last year.
6. **Golfer CRM** — per golfer: booking history, total spend, rounds per month,
   preferred times, no-shows, membership, staff notes; later targeted promos and
   retention.
7. **Visual polish** — softer charcoal rather than pure black, slightly
   transparent cards with subtle borders, consistent radii, subtle green on key
   numbers, small icons next to nav labels, better hover states and
   transitions, more whitespace. Don't overdo glass.
8. **Useful AI assistant** — "revenue this month vs October last year", "which
   times are consistently underbooked", "golfers who haven't played in 60
   days", "outstanding payments", "top products this week"; proactively flags
   unusual booking patterns and payment issues.

Its priority order: tee sheet, pro shop POS and revenue analytics (very high);
events, CRM, assistant and visual polish (high); advanced automation later.

## Tee sheet (from a screenshot of the current sheet)
Keep the 10-minute intervals, the four seat columns, the visible names and the
price on the left. Don't lose density.
1. Feel more like a scheduling app: more compact slots, slightly taller
   booking cards, more distinct colours, a fixed time and price column, and a
   sticky header for the seat columns. Less empty grid space.
2. Richer booking cards: name, group size and a payment indicator always
   visible; time, golfers, carts, booking total and booking source (online,
   phone…) on click.
3. A top toolbar: date with Today and a calendar, groups booked, slots
   available, expected revenue, New booking, Filters.
4. Consistent status colours: confirmed, checked in, pending, cancelled,
   blocked.
5. A right-side booking details panel: contact, all players, time and date,
   carts, payment status, notes; edit, cancel, check in and collect payment.
6. Views: grid, list, calendar.

Its build list for the tee sheet: toolbar; redesigned cards (group size,
payment and check-in status); the right-side drawer; status colours;
grid / list / calendar views.
