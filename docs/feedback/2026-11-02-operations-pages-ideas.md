# Operations pages ideas and feedback (saved for later, 2026-11-02)

Third part of the outside review Cam pasted, saved for discussion. It follows
`2026-11-02-dashboard-ideas.md` and `2026-11-02-proshop-analytics-ideas.md`.
Nothing here is decided; the opinions are the reviewer's, not Cam's.

## Overall
- A big jump: no longer looks like a basic booking app; it looks like software a
  GM or pro-shop manager could run the day on.
- Working: the off-white page with dark green accents, clean consistent type,
  well-spaced cards, professional without being generic corporate, and one
  design language across pages.
- Keep the visual direction; don't redesign it.

## Page by page
1. **Schedule**: functional and clear. Make the schedule visual (a timeline of
   available and booked times, not only settings fields); separate weekday and
   weekend pricing more clearly; add a section for blocked times, maintenance
   closures and shotgun starts; preview what golfers see when booking.
   Eventually one of the most important operational pages.
2. **Events** (one of the strongest sections): liked player tracking,
   checklists, payment tracking, pairings and groups, and public sign-up pages.
   Make the overview a command center: total registered, revenue collected,
   outstanding, spots left and tasks left at the top, with the details below. A
   potential major selling point.
3. **Event details and pairings**: pasting a spreadsheet, auto groups, starting
   holes and emailing pairings is excellent. Add drag-and-drop between groups,
   randomize pairings, stop duplicate registrations, export pairings (PDF,
   Excel), and a clean printable roster. Also track each player's payment even
   when the organizer pays one combined invoice — called out as particularly
   important.
4. **Members** (good foundation): liked the top numbers (active, not renewed,
   not linked, member rounds this year). Add membership revenue this year,
   expiry alerts, types and pricing, member utilization, and member pro-shop
   spend. A member profile with the whole relationship: rounds, purchases,
   payments, membership history and contact.
5. **Payments and payouts**: keep course revenue and Stripe payouts separate. A
   manager should see total revenue, collected, outstanding, refunds,
   processing fees, net revenue and actual bank payouts — especially with
   event, tee-time and membership money all flowing.
6. **Transactions list**: clean and scannable. Add filters (payment method, date
   range, type, status, refunds, employee); each transaction opens a detailed
   receipt and history.

## Its top five next
Pro shop POS, member profiles, tee sheet visualization, revenue analytics,
reporting and exports.

## Strategy it suggested
Think of GreenReserve as three connected products:
- **Course operations**: tee sheets, scheduling, events, tournaments,
  memberships.
- **Commerce**: pro shop POS, payments, inventory, merchandise, food and
  beverage.
- **Business intelligence**: revenue, customer spend, utilization,
  memberships, financial reporting.

The opportunity is connecting them: one dashboard showing rounds played,
tee-time revenue, pro shop sales, members who played and money collected. Focus
less on looks and more on the systems working together as one operating system
for the course.
